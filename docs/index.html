Tennessee tax sales

Buying Tennessee tax-sale property: what the sale price does not tell you

The bid is the cheapest number in the transaction. The redemption window and the county's own paperwork decide whether you made money.

A Tennessee tax sale looks simple from the outside: a parcel, a minimum bid, an auction date. In practice the number that decides the return is not on the sale list. It is the redemption window, the interest that accrues inside it, and whether the parcel you are bidding on is the parcel the county thinks it is. Buyers who lose money on their first one almost always lost it on something that was published, just not where they were looking.

The numbers that decide it

Advertised is not cleared

The advertised amount is the tax owed plus costs, not what the parcel fetches. The gap between the two, county by county, is the most useful number in the whole dataset - and nobody publishes it as a column.

The redemption window sets your clock

A tax sale is often a lien purchase, not an immediate deed. Until redemption runs out you are holding a claim, not a property, and your capital is parked in it.

Vacancy changes the window

Tennessee treats vacant and improved property differently for redemption, so the same parcel can be a one-year hold or a thirty-day one depending on its status. That single fact can move an annualised return by an order of magnitude.

Parcel identity is the silent killer

County parcel IDs are not standardised. The same parcel can appear under different formats across the assessor, the trustee and the sale list, and a bid on the wrong one is a bid on nothing.

Interest and fees are statutory, not negotiated

You are not pricing a deal. You are accepting a rate set by statute and hoping the timeline holds.

How to do it, in order

  1. Start from the county, not the list. Each Tennessee county runs its own process and its own calendar. The sale list is an output; the county trustee's office is the source.
  2. Match the parcel ID across three sources. Assessor, trustee and sale list. If the formats disagree, resolve it before bidding, not after.
  3. Check the status: vacant or improved. It changes your redemption window and therefore your annualised return. It is also the field most often wrong on a scraped list.
  4. Compute the hold, not the discount. Bid + costs + the months to redemption = the real price. A 60% discount held for two years is worse than a 20% discount held for three months.
  5. Write the exit before you bid. Redemption, resale, or build. If you cannot name the exit in one sentence, the discount is not the question.

Questions

Is a Tennessee tax sale a deed or a lien?

It depends on the county and the sale type, and the difference is everything: one gives you title after a waiting period, the other gives you a claim on a redemption payment. Confirm per county before bidding.

Why do advertised and cleared prices differ so much?

Because the advertised figure is the debt, and the clearing figure is what competing bidders decided the parcel is worth including the wait. The spread is the market's opinion of the county's timeline.

Can I research this without a subscription?

Yes, county by county. It is slow and it is the actual work - which is why a compiled calendar of sale dates and parcel counts is worth paying for.
